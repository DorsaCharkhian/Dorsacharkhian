About My Work

I’m a design researcher in Human-Computer Interaction and Human-AI Interaction, and a prospective PhD student for Fall 2027. I hold an M.S. in Digital Media from Drexel University and am completing an M.S. in Artificial Intelligence at Touro University. I use qualitative, participatory and speculative design methods to study how people interpret AI through their own language, culture and lived experience.

My current research direction asks how the contextual knowledge of immigrant and multilingual families can shape the AI systems they use for care, especially when parents make sense of their children’s health concerns alongside data from home devices. This builds on my master’s thesis, where I studied how international students experience a university portal through surveys, interviews and task-based testing, and designed D.A.V.E., a multilingual AI assistant. Students rejected “robotic” translation; they trusted AI only when its language felt culturally right.

My path into research began with craft. Trained in Islamic Art at Tabriz Art University, I designed objects, from children’s wooden toys to jewelry inspired by Iranian women artisans, before moving into interaction design, sensing-based installations and AI. That background shapes how I work: I start from the knowledge people already carry and turn it into prototypes and design directions that keep human relationships at the center of technology.
