Research

Current direction

I study how people interpret AI through their own language, culture and lived experience. My current direction asks how the contextual knowledge of immigrant and multilingual families can shape the AI systems they use for care, especially when parents make sense of their children’s health alongside data from home devices.

My master’s thesis studied how international students experience a university portal and led to D.A.V.E., a multilingual AI assistant. Students rejected “robotic” translation; they trusted AI only when its language felt culturally right.

Interests

Human-AI interaction for care and family health

How AI can support people’s sensemaking without replacing clinical care and human relationships.

Culturally and linguistically adaptive AI

Trust, accessibility and language in AI assistants for multilingual and immigrant communities.

Participatory, speculative and systemic design

Co-design, design fiction and service design, with growing work in personal sensing and interactive machine learning.
