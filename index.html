About My Work

I’m a design researcher in Human-Computer Interaction and Human-AI Interaction, and a prospective PhD student for Fall 2027. I study how people interpret AI through their own language, culture and lived experience, using qualitative, participatory and speculative design methods.

My current direction asks how the contextual knowledge of immigrant and multilingual families can shape the AI systems they use for care. It grows out of my master’s thesis at Drexel, where students rejected “robotic” translation and trusted AI only when its language felt culturally right, and out of a path that began with craft and Islamic art in Tabriz.
