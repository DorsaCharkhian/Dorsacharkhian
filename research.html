Research & publications

How people interpret AI through their own language, culture and lived experience.

Direction

My research sits in Human-AI Interaction and design research. I study how cultural and linguistic context shapes whether people understand and trust AI, and how that context can be designed into AI systems rather than treated as an afterthought.

My current direction asks how the contextual knowledge of immigrant and multilingual families can shape the AI systems they use for care, especially when parents make sense of their children’s health alongside data from home devices such as thermometers and wearables. I approach this through interviews, co-design and speculative design, and want to develop it further through personal sensing and interactive machine learning.
